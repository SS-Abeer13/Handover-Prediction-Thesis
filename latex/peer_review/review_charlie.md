### Summary

This is a BSc capstone thesis asking whether the next LTE handover can be forecast ahead of the reactive Event‑A3 trigger, from four live drive‑test campaigns (4 sessions, 10,260 one‑hertz samples, 957 RRC‑decoded handover commands). The headline methodological finding is a timestamp‑alignment defect in the one‑hertz export (76% of handovers show post‑handover cell identity in the row stamped before the command), whose correction collapses the one‑second AUPRC from 0.600 to 0.179. On the corrected, leakage‑audited pipeline, a discrete‑time hazard model (LightGBM) reaches AUPRC 0.179 at 1 s (6.8% prevalence floor, 2.6× lift, AUROC 0.777) and 0.475 at 5 s, evaluated under leave‑one‑campaign‑out (the only protocol treated as yielding independent units, n=4). The thesis also reports a splitting-protocol ablation (random-row splitting inflates AUPRC by 48%), a coherent multi-horizon hazard formulation, and a distribution-free risk-control analysis that is explicitly reported as failing to establish exchangeability across campaigns, alongside corrected characterisations of the deployed A3 configuration and the ping-pong rate's sensitivity to definitional choices.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The headline predictive-performance confidence intervals (e.g., AUPRC 0.179 [0.159, 0.204] at 1 s) are computed with exactly the block-bootstrap procedure that the thesis itself later proves is invalid on this data, yet this invalidated interval is retained as the reported uncertainty for the primary result throughout the abstract, Table 5.4, and the objectives discussion.
   **Where** — Table 5.4 ("Intervals are block-bootstrapped over 180s blocks... conditional on these four sessions"); contradicted by Section 5.3/5.4's own finding that "splitting by random 180-second blocks... still inflates it by 13%, because blocks of one continuous session are not independent of one another," and by the explicit retraction in Section 5.4: "resampling them deflates the standard error and manufactures a confidence interval narrower than the evidence supports... The claim is withdrawn."
   **Why it matters** — The retraction is applied only to the *paired arm-difference* comparisons in Section 5.4, not to the primary AUPRC/AUROC/ECE interval reported in Table 5.4 and repeated in the abstract and Chapter 7. But the same non-independence applies identically to that interval: it resamples non-independent 180 s blocks within held-out campaigns, so it understates uncertainty for the exact quantity (AUPRC 0.179) that is the thesis's central number. A reader is given a precise-looking interval ([0.159, 0.204]) for a result the authors elsewhere argue cannot be bounded that way with only 4 true independent units.
   **What would address it** — Either drop the block-bootstrap interval for Table 5.4 entirely and report only the per-campaign spread (as is already done, more honestly, in Table 5.10), or explicitly caveat every appearance of the block-bootstrap CI with the same disclaimer given in Section 5.4, so the reader does not treat [0.159, 0.204] as an honest precision statement.

2. **Issue** — The cross-campaign conformal risk-control result ("empirical holdout loss falling below α in 83% of 12 individual rotations") treats 12 calibrate/test rotations as if they were 12 roughly independent trials, when they are constructed as ordered pairs over only 4 underlying campaigns (4×3=12), so the effective sample size is 4, not 12.
   **Where** — Abstract; Section 5.5 ("Across the 12 cross-campaign rotations at α = 0.20... empirical loss falling below α in 83%, or 10 of 12, rotations"); Table 5.9.
   **Why it matters** — Because every rotation reuses one of only 4 campaigns as either the calibration or the test set, the 12 outcomes are heavily correlated (pseudoreplicated): a single unusually well- or poorly-behaved campaign can dominate several rotations at once. Reporting "83%" implies a resolution the underlying data cannot support, and risks being read by an operator or a future reviewer as a more reliable empirical success rate than 4 genuinely independent trials would justify. The thesis is careful about this exact issue everywhere else (explicitly refusing p-values from n=4), which makes the uncritical "83% of 12 rotations" framing here an inconsistency in the applied statistical standard.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly that the 12 rotations derive from only 4 independent campaigns and are not independent replicates; report the result broken out by which campaign served as calibration source (so the reader can see whether the 83% is driven by one or two campaigns), rather than pooling into a single percentage.

3. **Issue** — The "primary model" (LightGBM) reported throughout the abstract, contributions, and headline tables is selected from a leaderboard of 7 learners whose confidence intervals mostly overlap, but no pre-registration of LightGBM as the intended primary model prior to seeing results is stated, so the headline number carries an unquantified winner's-curse-style optimism from implicit selection.
   **Where** — Section 5.2 / Table 5.5 ("LightGBM is first at 0.179, MLP second at 0.173... the intervals of 10 of the arms overlap the leader's... The ordering of the top arms is not a finding of this thesis"); Section 4.7 designates LightGBM as "the primary model" without stating when that designation was fixed relative to the comparison.
   **Why it matters** — The thesis correctly disclaims that the *ranking* is not a finding, but it does not extend that caution to the *magnitude* of the reported headline AUPRC, which is still the single largest of the 7 point estimates and is the number carried into the abstract, objectives, and conclusion. If "primary model" was chosen after seeing Table 5.5 (which the freeze description in Section 3.2/5.2 does not clearly rule out for horizon-specific model selection, as opposed to the feature/formulation freeze), the reported effect size is the maximum of 7 correlated estimates rather than a single pre-specified test statistic.
   **What would address it** — State explicitly when and how LightGBM was designated primary relative to the comparison of Table 5.5 (before or after computing it), and, if after, report the headline number either as an average over learners or with an explicit multiplicity-adjusted interval rather than the raw maximum.

4. **Issue** — Numerous strong descriptive claims (A3 conversion rates, ping-pong rate across 9 definitional cells, the 76%/86.2%/93.2% contamination percentages, the 96%/2% attribution of AUPRC loss to feature blocks in Table 5.3) are reported as point estimates with no uncertainty interval, even though they are used to support mechanistic and normative conclusions ("the separation... survives all three [definitions] and is widest under the strictest").
   **Where** — Table 5.3 ("accounting for 95.8% of the reduction"), Table 5.14, Table 5.15/5.16, Section 5.1's contamination percentages.
   **Why it matters** — Some of these proportions are computed on small subgroups (e.g., n=15 boundary-crossing handovers, n=211 residual cases, n=93 inter-frequency handovers in Table 5.14) where sampling variability could be substantial, but the thesis reports them to one decimal place as if exact. This is inconsistent with the appropriately cautious statistical posture the thesis takes elsewhere (e.g., explicitly refusing significance tests on n=4 campaigns) and could mislead a reader into over-trusting fine-grained percentage differences computed on small n.
   **What would address it** — Add binomial confidence intervals (or at minimum, denominators alongside every percentage, which is mostly but not universally done) for proportions computed on subgroups below roughly n=50, particularly in Tables 5.3, 5.14, and the boundary-crossing evidence in Table A.6.

5. **Issue** — The thesis's evaluation protocol involves an extremely large number of researcher degrees of freedom exercised after all data were collected (180 s block length, 60 s purge, 10 s burst/quiet cutoff, 2 s blanking window, choice of 15 equal-mass calibration bins, choice of horizons), and while a sensitivity table (A.5) is provided, the thesis's own sensitivity analysis shows at least one result (the blocked-vs-LOCO gap) straddles zero and is explicitly "not established... in either direction," yet this same blocked protocol is elsewhere used as a practical recommendation ("which is what makes it a usable protocol when only one session is available").
   **Where** — Section 5.13 ("Post-hoc windows"); Table A.5; Section 5.3.
   **Why it matters** — Recommending the blocked+purge protocol as a practical substitute for leave-one-campaign-out, while simultaneously reporting that its own sensitivity analysis cannot establish the sign of its bias relative to LOCO, is an internally inconsistent recommendation: the thesis cannot both certify the protocol as "usable" and disclaim knowledge of whether it over- or under-estimates performance.
   **What would address it** — Either withdraw the practical recommendation to use blocked+purge splitting when only one session is available, or qualify it explicitly as "direction of bias unknown from this data" rather than implying it is a safe substitute.

6. **Issue** — No correction, or even discussion, is given for the number of statistical claims being tested informally across the thesis's many tables (7 learners × 5 horizons × 4 splitting protocols × 5+ calibration arms × 4 campaigns), beyond the single acknowledgment that "with four independent units... no [multiplicity correction] would be informative."
   **Where** — Section 4.7 (tuning-budget discussion); pervasive across Chapter 5.
   **Why it matters** — The "no correction possible with n=4" argument is reasonable for the specific paired-campaign comparisons the thesis restricts itself to, but it is used to justify skipping multiplicity consideration everywhere, including for comparisons that do not rely on the 4-campaign unit (e.g., the 22-cell definitional grid for ping-pong in Figure 5.15, the many single-feature AUROC comparisons in Table 5.12, or the ablation grid in Table 5.3). These have larger effective sample sizes where at least a qualitative acknowledgment of multiple comparisons (e.g., that 22 grid cells were computed and one convenient contrast is highlighted) would be appropriate.
   **What would address it** — Distinguish explicitly between comparisons genuinely limited by n=4 (where the "no correction possible" argument holds) and comparisons computed on the full row-level dataset (n in the thousands), and address multiplicity for the latter category even qualitatively.

7. **Issue** — Reproducibility of the reported statistics is deferred: code and derived data are "released on acceptance," meaning none of the specific numbers, contamination audits, or bootstrap procedures can currently be independently verified.
   **Where** — Section 7.3 ("Release of code and data"); Appendix C.
   **Why it matters** — Given how central small-sample statistical judgment calls are to this thesis's contribution (which arm counts as "coherent," which window length is used, how the 12 rotations are constructed), independent verification is unusually important here, more so than in a typical prediction paper, and currently impossible.
   **What would address it** — Release the derived frames and pipeline code alongside submission (or to reviewers under embargo) rather than contingent on acceptance, particularly the alignment-audit script and the conformal risk-control rotation construction.

### Minor concerns

- Section 2.3 contains a verbatim duplicated paragraph ("The narrow claim the audit supports, and the one made in this thesis, is therefore the following...") appearing twice in immediate succession — looks like a drafting/merge artifact rather than intentional repetition.
- Several reference entries are marked with editorial placeholders not resolved before this version ("[Author list to be completed from the ACM record before submission]," "[Full author list to be completed from the MDPI record before submission]," "[Venue to be confirmed]," "[Volume, issue and page range to be confirmed]") — these should not remain in a submission-ready manuscript.
- The "independent" collection used for generalisation in Section 5.7 is disclosed as sharing supervisor/department/instrument/operator with the primary campaigns (Section 6.6 COI declaration); this is handled honestly but the word "independent" is still used loosely in a few places (e.g., Table 5.11 caption) despite the text's own downgrading of the claim.
- Table 5.9's "Feasible rot." and "Bound met" column semantics are not clearly defined in the table itself and require cross-referencing Section 5.5's prose to interpret correctly.
- Given the thesis format (embedded OBE/course-outcome chapter, cost budget in BDT), substantial restructuring would be needed before this reads as a standalone journal submission; Chapter 6 in particular would need to be removed entirely.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the core contribution (the alignment audit and the honest, self-correcting statistical posture) is genuine and unusually rigorous for this scale of study, but the inconsistent application of the authors' own uncertainty-quantification standard to the headline result (Concern 1) and the pseudoreplication in the cross-campaign conformal evaluation (Concern 2) must be fixed before the reported precision of the central claims can be trusted.
