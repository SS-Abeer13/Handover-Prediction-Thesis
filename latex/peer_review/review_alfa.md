# Review — Reviewer alfa

## Summary

The thesis makes three linked claims: (1) a large fraction (76%) of handovers in the authors' own one-hertz LTE drive-test export are mislabeled by an end-of-second aggregation artifact, and correcting it collapses a headline AUPRC from 0.600 to 0.179; (2) a discrete-time hazard formulation predicts the next handover with modest skill (AUPRC 0.179 at 1s, 2.6× the prevalence floor; AUROC 0.777) while guaranteeing monotonic multi-horizon probabilities by construction; and (3) a distribution-free conformal risk bound on "miss rate" is reported honestly as failing to hold under cross-campaign deployment and as inexpressible at the 1s horizon for over a third of events. The alignment-audit finding (1) is genuinely valuable and unusually well-instrumented — it is corroborated on an independent multi-operator corpus and falsified against a public log with a different sampling mechanism. However, on close inspection the statistical apparatus behind claims (2) and (3) is applied inconsistently: the authors correctly diagnose, in one place, that their own 180-second block-bootstrap manufactures anti-conservative confidence intervals because blocks within a campaign are not independent — and withdraw a specific claim on that basis — but they do not apply the same skepticism to the primary AUPRC intervals or to the "exchangeable regime" conformal result that use the identical resampling machinery. With only n=4 truly independent units (campaigns) throughout, and per-campaign AUPRC spreads (0.132–0.233) wide enough to swallow the reported "2.6× lift," the headline predictive numbers should be read as far less precise than the tables imply. My overall stance: this is an unusually self-critical thesis with one strong contribution (the alignment audit) and a predictive/statistical contribution whose reported precision does not survive the paper's own logic applied consistently.

## Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The single largest correction in the thesis (the one-row alignment lag) was discovered by inspecting all four campaigns, including the campaign later reported as a held-out generalization test, which undermines the generalization claim built on it. `[Evidence — pre-registration/post-hoc]`
   **Where** — Section 3.2 ("the alignment audit of Section 5.1 ... was performed after all four campaigns, so no campaign is out of sample with respect to it"); Section 5.6 / Table 5.10 (leave-one-campaign-out results, including the "held-out" 4th/highway campaign).
   **Why it matters** — Objective O4 and Section 5.6 present the 4th campaign as a genuine out-of-distribution test of corridor and speed regime. But the feature-construction methodology itself (the lag, and by extension the entire feature pipeline that replaced the leaky one) was tuned using that same campaign's data before ever being "held out" for model evaluation. What survives as a clean LOCO test is only the model's hyperparameters, not the measurement/feature pipeline that determines what the model sees. This is exactly the class of leakage the thesis spends Section 5.1 teaching the reader to distrust (leakage into training examples that "every syntactic check passes"), applied one level up, to methodology rather than features.
   **What would address it** — Explicitly reframe the O4 claim as "the model generalizes across corridors given a feature pipeline validated on all available data" rather than "the system generalizes to a held-out corridor," or obtain a fifth campaign collected and audited without ever being used to shape the pipeline.

2. **Issue** — No hyperparameter search space, network depth/width, or learning-rate range is given anywhere for the five non-tree learners (logistic regression aside), so the headline numbers cannot be independently reproduced from the text alone. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — Section 4.7 ("Models compared") and Section 4.9 ("twenty random-search trials per outer fold") — no table of search bounds for MLP, TCN, Transformer, or GRU appears in the main text or Appendix B/C.
   **Why it matters** — Reproduction Notes (Appendix C) describes a pipeline of "numbered stages" and a "manifest," but that manifest is not shown, and code/data are only "committed on acceptance" — a promise with no defined venue or deadline attached here. A reader cannot check whether the reported AUPRC of 0.179 depends on a search space that happened to favor LightGBM, nor replicate Table 5.5's near-tie among seven learners.
   **What would address it** — Publish the actual search spaces (as a table) alongside the manuscript, not deferred to a hypothetical "acceptance."

3. **Issue** — The central falsification test for the aggregation-mechanism hypothesis relies on n=15 cases, and "0 of 15 contaminated" is reported with more confidence than that sample size supports. `[Evidence — statistical rigour, small-n]`
   **Where** — Section 5.1: "of the 15 handovers whose diagnostic completion interval crosses the boundary contaminates its row" / "an empirical hypothesis falsified by none of the 15 handovers here."
   **Why it matters** — If the true contamination rate in this regime were, say, 15%, the probability of observing zero contaminated cases in 15 trials by chance is still ≈0.87^15 ≈ 10%, i.e., not strong evidence of a hard zero. The same caution applies to the "isolated handovers" subsample (n=291) used to compute several downstream percentages (e.g., Table 5.1's "isolated" row), and to the fine-grained margin bins later in the same section (e.g., "903 handovers... 98.2% when 75 to 100 ms remain") where per-sub-bin counts are never stated, so the precision of the inferred "~60ms" logging-delay estimate cannot be assessed.
   **What would address it** — Report per-bin sample sizes for every contamination percentage in Section 5.1, and attach a confidence interval (even a simple Wilson interval) to the "0/15" and "60ms cliff" claims rather than presenting them as exact.

4. **Issue** — The claim that "no wireless conformal study ... reports a mobility-management case" rests on an unspecified literature screen with no stated search protocol, term list, or paper count — unlike the well-documented 108→22 screen used for the handover-prediction audit in Section 2.3. `[Novelty]`
   **Where** — Section 2.6 ("no wireless conformal study located in three screening passes reports a mobility-management case at all") and the parallel sentence in Section 2.9.
   **Why it matters** — This is exactly the kind of unverifiable negative-existence claim the paper otherwise takes pains to avoid (it audits the handover-prediction literature with a transparent, gradeable table). Readers cannot tell whether "three screening passes" means three keyword searches or three exhaustive database sweeps, so the strength of the "no prior mobility-management conformal work" claim — which underwrites part of contribution #5 — cannot be assessed independently.
   **What would address it** — Apply the same audit discipline used in Table 2.1/Section 2.3 to the conformal-prediction-in-wireless literature: state the search terms, date range, and paper count screened.

5. **Issue** — The thesis proves, in one place, that its own 180-second block-bootstrap produces anti-conservative confidence intervals due to intra-campaign correlation, and withdraws a claim on that basis — but continues to report and lean on statistically identical intervals, unflagged with the same caveat, for its two most important quantitative results: the primary AUPRC figure and the "exchangeable regime" conformal risk-control success. `[Evidence — statistical rigour]`
   **Where** — Section 5.4: "That version bootstrapped the paired differences over 180-second blocks ... Section 5.3 of this same thesis proves those blocks are not independent — splitting on them inflates one-second AUPRC by 13% — so resampling them deflates the standard error and manufactures a confidence interval narrower than the evidence supports. The claim is withdrawn." Contrast with Table 5.4's caption ("Intervals are block-bootstrapped over 180s blocks") reporting AUPRC 0.179 [0.159, 0.204] at 1s, and with Section 5.5's "exchangeable reference regime" (57 pooled 180-second blocks, calibration n≈43) reporting a successful conformal bound (realized loss 0.168 at α=0.20) using the same block-level resampling that Section 5.3 independently showed inflates apparent performance by 13% when used for model splitting.
   **Why it matters** — This is not a hypothetical concern; the paper's own Section 5.3 experiment (identical data, identical folds, only the splitting/resampling unit varied) is the direct evidence that 180-second blocks share enough structure (cells, traffic, trajectory) to bias any interval built from resampling them. The primary claim of the thesis — "AUPRC 0.179 at one second... against 0.116 for the A3 signal-margin proxy" — is defended with an interval built from exactly the mechanism the authors elsewhere call out as anti-conservative. The per-campaign spread the authors do report honestly (Table 5.10: AUPRC 0.132 to 0.233 across the four held-out campaigns) is wide enough that a properly cluster-aware (n=4) interval would very plausibly overlap the A3-proxy baseline and would certainly be far wider than [0.159, 0.204]. Likewise, the "exchangeable regime" conformal success in Section 5.5 is exactly the kind of within-corpus, block-level exchangeability the paper's own splitting-ladder experiment discredits as a proxy for genuine independence; labeling it "exchangeable" and reporting it as meeting the target (0.168 ≤ 0.20) risks being read as "conformal risk control worked here" when the same logic that invalidated Table 5.8's comparison applies with equal force.
   **What would address it** — Either (a) recompute the primary AUPRC/ECE intervals and the "exchangeable regime" conformal bound using a resampling unit the paper itself considers defensible (i.e., the 4 campaigns, accepting that this yields uninformative or very wide intervals, as the paper already does for paired-difference tests), or (b) attach to Table 5.4, Table 5.5, and Section 5.5 the same explicit anti-conservatism caveat given to Table 5.8, rather than the softer "conditional on these four sessions" hedge currently used only for the headline numbers.

- Table 5.9's formatting embeds a footnoted asterisk directly into a data cell ("100%*0%"), and reports a degenerate operating point (λ=0, 100% alarm rate, "0% realised loss") in a results table without strongly marking it as vacuous — it is flagged in a footnote but easy to miss.
- Handover counts (957, 938, 935, 934) recur across chapters with slightly different denominators depending on quality-control stage; a single master reconciliation table (analogous to Appendix B.1's feature-count table) would remove the need to re-derive which N applies where.
- Equation (4.8)'s claim about which horizon suffers the larger distortion under class reweighting ("largest at the shortest horizon" vs "largest at the longest") is a first-order approximation for small λ, presented with more confidence than the derivation supports, and is never checked empirically.
- Chapter 6 (Outcome-Based Education / course-outcome mapping) is program-accreditation boilerplate that would need to be removed entirely before any journal submission; worth flagging given the stated goal of the surrounding project.
- Checks that produced nothing of note: cherry-picking (Table 5.18/Figure 5.20's nine cases are explicitly drawn uniformly at random with a fixed seed — good practice, not cherry-picked); baseline strength (the A3 signal-margin proxy and the domain-adaptation baselines appear to have been implemented in reasonably good faith, and the CORAL null result is consistent with the cited benchmarking literature); competing-interest disclosure (Section 6.6 discloses the supervisor's co-authorship of the "independent" dataset and comparator plainly, and appropriately downgrades the word "independent").

## Belief update

I updated moderately on one specific, narrow proposition: *published drive-test handover-prediction accuracy figures built on one-hertz aggregate exports are at meaningful risk of being inflated by an aggregation-boundary labeling artifact that standard grouped-split leakage checks do not catch.* The magnitude here (AUPRC 0.600 → 0.179, a 3.4× collapse) and the cross-instrument corroboration (the NUWiNS downsample-both-ways test) make this a credible, generalizable methodological finding independent of everything else in the thesis. I did not update toward believing that the discrete-time hazard formulation or the conformal risk-control machinery add practical value here: the hazard model does not outrank the free monotone-projection controls, the conformal bound is explicitly not established cross-campaign, and I now trust the headline AUPRC number itself less than the tables suggest, given finding #5 above.

## Verdict

**Major revision.** The alignment-audit contribution is strong and worth publishing, but the primary predictive and risk-control numbers are defended with confidence intervals the paper's own Section 5.3 analysis shows to be anti-conservative when applied to this data's block structure — that inconsistency (Major concern #5) must be resolved, and the headline AUPRC/conformal claims re-expressed with honest n=4-cluster uncertainty, before the quantitative claims can be taken at face value.
