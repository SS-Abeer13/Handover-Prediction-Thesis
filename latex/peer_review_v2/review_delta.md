# Peer Review — Reviewer delta

## Summary

This undergraduate thesis asks whether the next LTE handover can be forecast from handset-observable measurements, at what lead time, and with what statistical guarantee, using four drive-test campaigns (957 decoded handover commands, 10,260 one-hertz samples) on a single commercial operator in Dhaka/Gazipur. The headline methodological finding is a timestamp-alignment defect in the one-hertz measurement export: in 76% of handovers the export row nominally preceding the command already reflects the post-handover serving cell, which inflates one-second AUPRC from a corrected 0.179 to an artefactual 0.600. After correcting this leak, the authors pose the forecasting task as discrete-time survival (per-interval hazards multiplied into horizon probabilities, guaranteeing coherence by construction), evaluate under leave-one-campaign-out cross-validation, and report modest predictive performance (AUPRC 0.179 at 1 s, 0.475 at 5 s, against prevalence floors of 6.8% and 26.1%), explicitly concluding the resulting warning system is not deployable and that the paper's real contribution is the leakage-audited measurement/evaluation framework rather than a working predictor. Conformal risk control is applied but shown to fail its distribution-free guarantee across campaigns because exchangeability is violated, and several corrected characterisations of the deployed A3 configuration (conversion rate, offset distribution, ping-pong rate) are reported with explicit sensitivity to definitional choices.

## Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The single largest determinant of every reported result (the one-row export lag, discovered via the alignment audit) was decided only after inspecting all four campaigns, including the nominally "held-out" Campaign 4, so the leave-one-campaign-out protocol does not actually test out-of-sample generalisation of the analysis pipeline as a whole — only of hyperparameters within a feature/formulation pipeline that was itself chosen using the full dataset.
   **Where** — Section 3.2 ("the alignment audit ... was performed retrospectively after all four campaigns had been acquired"); Section 5.2 ("The alignment audit and the feature lag were themselves decided after all four campaigns were in hand ... the honest description of everything after it is exploratory development rather than confirmatory evaluation").
   **Why it matters** — Objective O4 and the framing of Campaign 4 as an independent test of corridor/speed generalisation (Section 3.2: "it is the only campaign that differs in both corridor and speed regime, so it is reported as a held-out campaign throughout") rest on a design in which the analyst had already seen the answer for that campaign before choosing the preprocessing that produces the answer. This is the textbook experimental-design failure of "peeking" at held-out data during pipeline development — the freeze manifest protects the learner and hyperparameter search, but not the much larger-effect decision (236% swing) of how features are constructed. The paper's abstract and Table 5.4/5.10 nonetheless present confidence intervals and per-campaign spreads in the same register as confirmatory results.
   **What would address it** — Explicitly downgrade every LOCO-based generalisation claim (not just the model-ranking ones) to "exploratory" in the abstract and conclusion, not only in Section 5.2; or, better, re-run the entire pipeline (including alignment audit and feature design) on Campaigns 1–3 only, with Campaign 4 truly untouched until a single final confirmatory pass, and report both the pre-registered and the actual (adjusted) protocol side by side.

2. **Issue** — The mechanistic explanation for the leak (end-of-second aggregation causing serving-cell contamination) is validated by a "falsification test" (0/15 boundary-crossing handovers) that is derived from, and applied to, the same 938-handover corpus that generated the hypothesis, making it closer to a confirmatory re-description of the data than an independent test.
   **Where** — Section 5.1 ("Of the 15 handovers whose completion crosses the second boundary, m<0, not one has the target cell on the row stamped t (0/15; 95% Wilson score interval [0.0%, 20.4%])").
   **Why it matters** — With n=15 and a one-sided Wilson upper bound of 20.4%, the data are compatible with a non-trivial contamination rate under the proposed mechanism; framing this as evidence that "falsifies" a competing account overstates what a same-sample, small-n check can show. The cross-instrument replications (XCAL 3-operator corpus, NUWiNS downsampling experiment) are genuinely independent and much stronger evidence — but the in-house "prediction test" that the text leans on most heavily for causal attribution is not.
   **What would address it** — De-emphasize the 15-handover in-corpus check relative to the cross-instrument replications, or explicitly pre-register a mechanism prediction on a chronologically or instrument-wise disjoint subset before looking at it, and report the Wilson interval's practical implication (up to ~1-in-5 boundary cases could be contaminated) rather than the point estimate alone.

3. **Issue** — The only genuinely out-of-corridor/out-of-speed test (Campaign 4, highway) has speed and corridor 100% confounded by construction, yet the paper still reports Objective O4 as "met" in the discussion.
   **Where** — Section 5.6 ("vehicle velocity ... and geographic corridor ... are completely collinear and confounded"); Section 5.13.2 (same); Section 5.12 ("Objective O4 is met at two distances: leave-one-campaign-out AUROC between 0.765 and 0.805, including 0.769 on the highway corridor").
   **Why it matters** — A single-route, single-speed-regime holdout cannot separate "does the model generalise to unfamiliar radio geometry" from "does it generalise to a different speed distribution," which is exactly the O4 question ("both to a corridor and speed regime held out entirely"). Declaring the objective "met" when the experimental design cannot isolate the manipulated factor is an overclaim, even though the confound is honestly disclosed elsewhere in the same document.
   **What would address it** — Either soften the O4 verdict to "partially met, confound unresolved" throughout (not just in the threats-to-validity appendix), or add the controlled experiment the authors themselves propose in Section 7.3 (same urban loop driven at multiple speeds) before claiming generalisation across "speed regimes" as an achieved result.

4. **Issue** — Nearly every quantitative claim in the thesis (network characterisation, ping-pong rates, A3 conversion rates, per-campaign spreads, conformal feasibility floors) is computed on n=4 non-randomly-selected campaigns from one operator, one city pair, and one measurement window, with no test-retest replication (e.g., repeated drives of the same route on different days) to check whether cross-campaign variance reflects the manipulated factors (corridor, speed) or uncontrolled temporal drift (network load, possible eNodeB parameter changes, device state).
   **Where** — Section 3.2 (four campaigns, single instrument, no repeat-route control); Section 3.5 ("the configuration set is stable across all four campaigns" is asserted from the same four sessions it is meant to validate); Section 5.13.4 (acknowledges n=4 limits inference to effect sizes).
   **Why it matters** — The paper is careful to avoid formal significance claims from n=4, which is commendable, but many descriptive claims are still stated with more confidence than an unreplicated, uncontrolled n=4 convenience sample supports — e.g., "the fastest campaign is also the most return-prone" (Section 5.10) is presented as a documented empirical pattern rather than what it is: one observation confounded with route, cell density, carrier mix, and time of day, exactly as the authors themselves note two sentences later. Without any within-condition replicate, there is no way to estimate how much of the observed cross-campaign spread is measurement noise versus a real corridor/speed effect.
   **What would address it** — Add at least one repeated-route drive under matched conditions to establish a noise floor for cross-campaign comparisons, or explicitly bound every cross-campaign descriptive claim with a statement that zero replicates exist to distinguish signal from session-level nuisance variation.

5. **Issue** — The "independent" external validation (Shafi et al. dataset, Section 5.7) shares the supervisor as a co-author, the same department, city, operator, and instrument as this thesis, which weakens its value as a test of generalisation despite being properly disclosed as a conflict of interest.
   **Where** — Section 5.7; Section 6.6 ("Declaration of competing interests").
   **Why it matters** — The paper's own framing repeatedly stresses that this "tests collection, route and time — not operator, and not radio access technology" (Section 5.7), which is accurate, but the abstract and contributions list ("Acknowledgements... the publicly released LTE drive-test dataset used for the independent check") use "independent" in a way a reader could easily over-read as arms-length external validation. Combined with concern 3, this means the thesis has essentially zero fully external evidence for generalisation beyond one lab's instrumentation and one operator.
   **What would address it** — Rename this experiment consistently as an "affiliated replication" rather than "independent collection" in the abstract/contributions summary (the disclosure already exists in Chapter 6, but the framing in Chapter 1 and the abstract is more generous than the COI section supports), and flag the absence of any truly third-party dataset as a first-order external validity limitation rather than a secondary one.

## Minor concerns

- The feature count bookkeeping (89/17/6 = 112 main features vs. 109 lagged columns vs. 122 on-disk columns) requires cross-referencing three different tables (4.3, 5.3, B.1) to reconcile; a single canonical table earlier in the text would reduce reader error risk.
- The abstract reports the block-bootstrap 95% CI ([0.159, 0.204]) without the caveat, stated later in Section 5.2, that these within-session intervals are "anti-conservative" — a reader who only reads the abstract could mistake them for a valid uncertainty quantification.
- Multiplicity policy is inconsistent: no correction is applied across the seven-learner leaderboard or the many paired-arm comparisons (justified as "descriptive"), but Holm–Bonferroni is applied only to the four Hawkes-process goodness-of-fit tests (Section 4.7, 5.10) — the rationale for treating one family of tests as confirmatory and all others as descriptive is asserted rather than argued.
- The "trigger-episode" one-to-one matching rule ("matched to its corresponding ... via most proximate associated report," Section 5.9) is described narratively but not given as reproducible pseudocode/algorithm; given how much the A3 conversion-rate conclusion depends on this matching rule, this is a reproducibility gap.
- Code and data release is promised "on acceptance" (Section 7.3) rather than provided with submission, so none of the numeric claims in this review could be independently checked against the pipeline.
- Minor terminology looseness: "falsified by none of the 15 handovers" (Abstract) is stronger language than the Wilson interval it is based on supports (see Major concern 2).

## Verdict

**Major revision** — the underlying work is unusually careful and self-critical for a BSc thesis (the leakage audit and its cross-instrument replication are genuine contributions), but the experimental design still lets the "held-out" campaign's independence, the O4 generalisation claim, and the causal mechanism test lean on post-hoc, same-sample, or confounded evidence more than the paper's own confident framing acknowledges, and these gaps need to be either fixed or consistently down-weighted throughout the abstract, contributions list, and discussion before the claims match the evidence.
