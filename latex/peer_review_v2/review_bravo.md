### Summary

This bachelor's thesis (IUT, Bangladesh) asks whether the next LTE Event-A3 handover can be forecast ahead of the reactive trigger, using one-hertz drive-test telemetry and decoded RRC signalling from four campaigns (957 handover commands, 2.9 hours). Its actual headline finding is methodological rather than predictive: an "alignment audit" reveals that in 76% of handovers the pre-handover export row already carries the post-handover serving cell, and correcting this collapses one-second AUPRC from an initially reported 0.600 to 0.179 (against a 6.8% prevalence floor, 2.6× lift, AUROC 0.777). Built on the corrected data, the authors pose multi-horizon handover forecasting as discrete-time survival (a per-interval hazard model whose horizon outputs are coherent by construction), compare four splitting protocols (showing random-row and block splits inflate AUPRC by 48% and 13% respectively over leave-one-campaign-out), and apply conformal risk control as a "calibration diagnostic," concluding that no operating point measured is deployable and that the paper's contribution is the leakage-audited measurement framework itself rather than a working predictor.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The thesis's central novelty claim for its conformal-risk-control contribution ("no published or preprint study... evaluated conformal prediction or conformal risk control for real-time handover event forecasting") is established only by a self-run keyword search, and the search appears to have missed the literature that is *most* directly relevant to the exact problem the thesis frames as its "boundary result": conformal prediction under non-exchangeability / distribution shift for sequential or temporally structured data (e.g., the "adaptive conformal inference under distribution shift" line of work, and "conformal prediction beyond exchangeability" style results using weighted/relaxed exchangeability). 
   **Where** — Section 2.6 ("Distribution-free uncertainty quantification") and Section 4.10/5.5, where the campaign-level exchangeability violation is treated as a freshly discovered structural limit rather than an instance of a well-studied problem with existing (if imperfect) remedies such as weighted conformal calibration or online/adaptive threshold updating.
   **Why it matters** — The paper's Contribution 5 is explicitly framed as novel ("the identification of the exchangeability unit... and the empirical demonstration of non-exchangeability across campaigns constitute the focus of this contribution"), but if the general problem and candidate solutions already exist in the conformal-prediction literature under different application labels, the contribution reduces to *instantiating* a known diagnostic on a new dataset — a legitimate but much narrower claim than "boundary result" language conveys, and the thesis never attempts (or cites an attempt at) the weighted/adaptive remedies that the mainstream conformal-under-shift literature would suggest as the next step, despite devoting a "Recommendations" item to it.
   **What would address it** — Run (or explicitly discuss and rule out) a weighted-conformal or covariate-shift-adjusted calibration baseline against the campaign-shift problem, and reframe the novelty claim narrowly as "first application to handover forecasting" rather than implying the underlying statistical problem itself was previously unaddressed; the literature-search methodology (databases, exact strings, date range) should be reported with enough detail (or as a PRISMA-style flow) that a reader can audit whether directly relevant conformal-under-shift papers were excluded by the query design rather than because they don't exist.

2. **Issue** — The claim of being the first to pose handover prediction as a *learned, covariate-conditional, per-sample* discrete-time hazard model rests on an audit of only 22 papers drawn from a domain-restricted, 2020–2026 screen of the handover/RLF/next-cell prediction literature, while the specific machinery being used (binary-cross-entropy-over-expanded-bins as a discrete hazard, i.e., the "continuation ratio" trick) is textbook-standard in adjacent applied ML domains — churn prediction, predictive maintenance / remaining-useful-life estimation, hard-drive failure forecasting — none of which is searched or cited.
   **Where** — Section 2.5 ("Survival analysis as the appropriate formulation") and Section 2.9 ("Research gap"); Contribution 4 in Section 1.6.
   **Why it matters** — The paper explicitly narrows its own claim ("Nothing else about the formulation is claimed as new"), which is honest, but then spends a full chapter (4.3–4.5) presenting a near-trivial monotonicity proof and a censoring likelihood that the paper itself later admits is "inert" on this dataset (Section 5.4) as if it were a load-bearing methodological contribution. Readers from the survival-ML community (rather than the wireless community the audit is scoped to) would recognize this formulation as off-the-shelf, which the novelty framing in the abstract and Chapter 1 does not adequately signal.
   **What would address it** — Cite the applied discrete-time-hazard-via-classification literature outside medicine/wireless (e.g., predictive maintenance / RUL, subscriber-churn survival models) to properly locate the technique's origin, and compress the "technical advantage" framing (Section 4.3) to match the paper's own later concession that this is a convenience/calibration argument, not a structural one.

3. **Issue** — The independent-validation datasets used to support generalization claims (Objective O4, Section 5.7) are not independent in the sense a reader would assume, and this materially weakens the external-validity story.
   **Where** — Section 5.7 and the Declaration of Competing Interests in Section 6.6: the "independently collected public dataset" [38] and the reimplemented RL comparator [39] were produced by the same supervisor's research group, in the same department, on the same operator/city/instrument.
   **Why it matters** — The transfer experiment is explicitly restricted to a reduced 6-feature signalling-only representation because the public dataset lacks synchronous radio/GPS data, and the paper concedes outright that "the primary model has never been evaluated outside the four campaigns it was built on." Given this, Objective O4 ("test whether the result generalises... to an independently collected public dataset") is met only in a much weaker sense than the objective's own wording implies, and the shared-supervisor provenance (disclosed, to the authors' credit) means this is not an arms-length replication.
   **What would address it** — Either drop "independent" from the framing of this experiment and describe it plainly as an intra-lab, same-network transfer check, or acquire truly external data (different operator, different research group, ideally different country) before claiming any generalization beyond the four campaigns.

4. **Issue** — With only n = 4 independent units (campaigns), the thesis repeatedly reports directional statistics ("4 of 4 campaigns favour the hazard arm," "3 of 4 campaigns," etc.) that function rhetorically as confirmatory evidence even though the text elsewhere correctly states that a sign test on 4 units cannot fall below p = 0.125.
   **Where** — Table 5.8 and surrounding text in Section 5.4; Section 5.13.4 ("Statistical conclusion validity").
   **Why it matters** — The paper is commendably explicit that no significance claim is being made, but the "k of 4" framing is used at least half a dozen times across the results chapter as the operative evidence for claims like "the calibration result holds," which risks being read by a reader (or cited by future work) as stronger support than four correlated, non-independent-route observations can bear. This is compounded by the fact that the four campaigns are not a random sample of anything — they are one operator's four drives chosen by the authors, so even a clean "4 of 4" is a case study, not a population estimate.
   **What would address it** — Either drop directional vote-counting language entirely in favor of the raw per-campaign numbers (which the paper already reports), or explicitly caveat every "k of 4" statement with a reminder that four is not just "underpowered" but essentially anecdotal for ranking purposes.

5. **Issue** — The evaluation protocol (180 s blocks, 60 s purge, 10 s burst cutoff, 2 s blanking window, the one-row lag itself, and the decision to treat Campaign 4 as held-out) was substantially finalized or revised *after* all four campaigns had already been collected and, in the case of the alignment audit, after a full draft of the thesis had been written around the uncorrected numbers.
   **Where** — Section 3.2 ("the alignment audit... was performed retrospectively after all four campaigns had been acquired"); Section 5.2 ("The alignment audit and the feature lag were themselves decided after all four campaigns were in hand"); Appendix C.
   **Why it matters** — The paper is unusually candid about this and reports a sensitivity ladder (Table A.5) to show robustness, which is good practice — but a git-commit manifest that postdates data collection is not equivalent to genuine pre-registration, and the paper's own Section 3.2 acknowledges the freeze was committed to an *internal* repository, "not registered with an external independent trial registry." Given how many quantitative thresholds were chosen with the data already in view, garden-of-forking-paths risk is real even though each individual choice looks defensible in isolation.
   **What would address it** — Register future protocols externally before data collection (as the authors themselves recommend for future work), and in this thesis, more sharply distinguish "confirmatory" from "exploratory" numbers throughout Chapter 5 rather than relegating that distinction to a single sentence in Section 5.2.

6. **Issue** — Full code and data are not available for review; they are promised only "on acceptance," and even then only as derived frames and decoded events rather than raw logs.
   **Where** — Section 7.3 ("Recommendations for future work" / "Release of code and data") and Appendix C.
   **Why it matters** — Nearly every number in the thesis depends on a bespoke, non-trivial signalling-decoding pipeline (VarMeasConfig timeline replay, measId resolution, the end-of-second aggregation inference) that the paper itself shows was wrong in an earlier draft (the flat-union parser misclassified 99.4% of reports as A3). Given that history of pipeline bugs materially changing headline numbers, the inability to inspect the actual decoding code at review time is a significant reproducibility gap for a paper whose core contribution is methodological rigor.
   **What would address it** — Provide the decoding and analysis code (even if raw vendor logs cannot be redistributed) to reviewers under a confidential access arrangement rather than deferring release entirely to post-acceptance.

### Minor concerns

- Table 3.2 is captioned "The four measurement campaigns **after quality control**," but its "Handover commands" column sums to 957 (307+176+297+177), matching the *raw* decoded total stated elsewhere, not the 938 post-QC figure used throughout Chapter 5 (cf. - The abstract and Chapter 1 quote a 0/15 falsification test with a 95% Wilson CI of [0.0%, 20.4%] as if it strongly supports the end-of-second aggregation hypothesis; given n=15, this interval is wide enough that the "empirical hypothesis falsified by none of the 15 handovers" framing somewhat overstates the strength of that particular test in isolation (the corroborating downsampling experiments on NUWiNS data are considerably stronger evidence and should be foregrounded over the n=15 result).
- Table 5.9's footnote logic (feasibility floors, asterisked rows) is dense enough that it would benefit from a worked numeric example in the main text rather than only in the table footnote.
- Minor terminology drift: "trigger episode" is defined and re-defined slightly differently across Sections 1.2, 2.4, and 5.9 (loose vs. strict matching); a single canonical definition box would help.
- Chapter 6 (Outcome-Based Education) is retained in the text supplied even though the front matter says it is "omitted when compiled in journal mode" — for a journal submission this chapter (and the declaration/approval pages) should be stripped entirely.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the leakage-audit and protocol-comparison findings are a genuinely valuable, honestly reported methodological contribution, but the novelty framing around conformal risk control and the survival formulation overreaches relative to adjacent literatures that appear not to have been searched, the "independent" validation is not arms-length, and n=4 campaigns cannot support several of the directional claims as currently worded.
