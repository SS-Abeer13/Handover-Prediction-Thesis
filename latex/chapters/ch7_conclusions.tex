\chapter{Conclusions}\label{ch:7}


\section{Summary of findings}
\label{sec:7_1}

This thesis set out to determine whether the next LTE handover can be forecast from the measurements a handset already reports, how far in advance, and with what guarantee. On four drive-test campaigns conducted on a live commercial network in Dhaka and Gazipur --- four continuous sessions, 10,260 samples and 957 handover commands whose ground truth is decoded from RRC signalling (yielding 938 quality-controlled commands inside retained session blocks, and 934--937 horizon-evaluable events under lead boundaries) --- the answer is a qualified yes, and the qualification is the first finding.

The measurement grid must be aligned to the event clock before anything else is believed. In 76\% of handovers the one-hertz export row stamped before the handover command already carries the post-handover serving cell. Lagging every export feature by one row moves the one-second AUPRC of the same fixed-parameter model from 0.604 to 0.156, and the dwell feature's own AUROC from 0.873 to 0.664. While Campaign 4 serves as an out-of-sample holdout for model weights, it was part of the retrospective diagnostic exploration that derived the one-row lag repair; C4 is therefore an out-of-sample test for model parameter fitting, but not a blind holdout for pipeline feature engineering. A fifth capture on a second operator, recorded after the freeze, closes that gap for the central claim: there the row at the command already carries the target in 67\% of handovers against 5\% one row earlier, and the unlagged model is inflated 4.3-fold. The audit costs three lines of code and is the recommendation this thesis makes most strongly.

The next handover is predictable, modestly. Gradient-boosted trees under a discrete-time hazard formulation reach pooled AUPRC 0.169 across campaigns (observed range over held-out campaigns [0.125, 0.223], not a prediction interval for a new campaign; within-session block-bootstrap 95\% interval [0.150, 0.196], anti-conservative) at one second against a 6.8\% floor (2.5x, AUROC 0.773) and AUPRC 0.454 at five seconds (1.7x, LOCO spread [0.385, 0.531]), against AUPRC 0.114 for the implemented A3 signal-margin proxy scored as a predictor. On the blind second-operator capture the same model reaches 0.137 (2.3x, AUROC 0.773), inside the four-campaign range. There, however, logistic regression reaches 0.261 and the history-only models also score higher, so the lead of the gradient-boosted trees is a within-operator result and ``primary model'' is a label for the learner carried through the analyses, not a finding. Objective O4 is partially met with structural confounding, because vehicle speed and corridor geography are collinear on the highway campaign, and cross-corpus evaluation on a same-institution public dataset collected in the same metropolitan area on the same operator by an earlier research cohort in our department [38] was constrained to common L3 signalling features.

The unit of splitting is worth more than the model. Against leave-one-campaign-out, random-row splitting inflates the one-second AUPRC by 49\% and random 180-second blocks of the same session by 12\%; the inflation is architecture-dependent, so a careless protocol reorders the leaderboard rather than merely raising it.

A single hazard model makes the five horizons mutually consistent, and the correct comparison for that property is a monotone projection rather than raw independent classifiers. Independent heads violate ordering on 38.9\% of rows; a cumulative-maximum projection, isotonic calibration composed with that projection, and the hazard model all reach zero. The hazard model calibrates better than the projections that cost nothing (0.037 against 0.043 at one second) and worse than the isotonic composite (0.015), which spends a calibration split this dataset cannot spare.

Conformal risk control evaluated over rows yields an empirical miss rate at or below the target within pooled 180-second blocks (0.171 at $\alpha = 0.20$, at a 48\% alarm rate); calibrating on one campaign and evaluating on another, the pooled empirical mean miss loss across the 12 pairwise campaign rotations was 0.156 at target $\alpha = 0.20$, with 8 of the 12 rotations at or below the target; tested on the second operator, the per-unit miss loss was 0.03 to 0.08 at the same target, a conservative result. However, cross-campaign distribution-free guarantees cannot be certified because macro-environmental shifts violate exchangeability, and the 12 pairwise permutations exhibit pseudoreplication over $n=4$ physical sessions. Over handovers, which is what the objective promised, no useful bound is expressible at one second, because 36.4\% of commands have no usable row in their lead window (under the two-second post-handover blanking policy and boundary exclusions) and are missed whatever the threshold. The grid together with the evaluation policy, rather than model discrimination alone, is the binding constraint on what a study like this may promise. All of these conformal results are empirical calibration diagnostics of a procedure whose scores come from four fitted models; none is an operating guarantee for a deployed predictor.

Two properties of the deployed network were characterised, both corrected against an earlier draft, and a third quantity was shown to have no unit-free value at all. The share of A3 reports with no matched command within a two-second attribution window reads 63\% per transmitted report, 30\% per trigger episode when overlapping episodes may claim the same command, and 73\% once each command is matched to exactly one episode; what survives every unit is the separation between the mobility configuration and the monitoring configurations (37\% unmatched against 95\%). Unmatched is a counting statement, not an observation that the network declined a report. Weighted by handovers rather than by configurations, 75\% of A3-triggered handovers fire under a positive offset. And the ping-pong rate on one fixed set of 957 handover commands moves from 26.3\% to 43.8\% depending on three definitional choices that published work routinely leaves unstated. The definitional findings hold on the second operator too; the substantive patterns do not. There Event A5 drives most handovers, 87\% of A3 trigger episodes go unmatched one-to-one, and ping-pong is more frequent across carriers than within one --- the reverse of the first operator.

Underlying all of these is the most compact statement of what this work found: on drive-test data, the protocol and the timestamp semantics decide the result before the model does. The second-operator test adds a corollary: the timestamp semantics travel with the instrument, while the best model and the network's habits stay with the operator.


\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:7_2}

The limits of the present study are restated here because they determine what follows from it.

The evidence base is four continuous sessions, one operator, four days and one radio access technology, with one further 17-minute session on a second operator. The deployed configuration is stable across all four campaigns, so the cross-regime transfer of Section~\ref{sec:5_6} rests on a single configuration set. The second-operator session tests what a second corridor cannot, and it shows that the model ranking and the carrier pattern of ping-pong change with the network. One short session cannot say by how much: it has 52 positive rows at one second and six 180-second blocks, and its intervals are within-session. Neighbour features depend on a parser fix made after the four campaigns were analysed (Section~\ref{sec:3_3}); every model analysis was re-run with it, and it changed the headline four-campaign results by at most 0.011 AUPRC. All captures were conducted using a single commercial drive-test handset running a single Accuver XCAL firmware build; the NUWiNS experiment shows that end-of-second aggregation is sufficient to produce the artefact, not that this exporter applies it, and handset-specific baseband logging, timestamp rounding or diagnostic buffer commit latencies cannot be separated without native-rate, multi-device, multi-firmware replication. The operating points of Appendix A show predictive signal but not usefulness for a particular network action: they were not set against an explicit alarm budget or minimum useful lead time, the forecast does not name the target cell, and the delay before a feature becomes available to a live predictor was not measured. The combination of the one-hertz export and the two-second post-handover blanking policy caps event-level detection and fixes the information cut-off one sample period before the prediction instant, so every figure here is conservative by that margin and exact semantics await a native-rate export.

\section{Recommendations for future work}
\label{sec:7_3}

Seven items follow from those limits, in the order the limits dictate.

\paragraph{A native-rate export and a published alignment statement.} The single most valuable follow-up is a re-export of the same captures at the message rate rather than at one hertz. Section~\ref{sec:5_1} infers the write rule from its consequences and shows on a native-rate corpus collected elsewhere that the inferred rule is sufficient to produce the effect; a native-rate export of these captures, compared under documented start-, end- and instantaneous-sampling rules, would replace inference with observation, separate leak removal from the one-second ageing penalty, and allow horizons below one second to be modelled rather than approximated.

\paragraph{Multi-device and multi-firmware cross-instrumentation.} The alignment artifact in Section~\ref{sec:5_1} was audited on a single commercial handset running a single Accuver XCAL firmware release. The NUWiNS experiment shows that end-of-second aggregation is sufficient to produce the artefact on XCAL data; multi-device replication across distinct modem chipsets (e.g., Qualcomm Snapdragon, MediaTek Dimensity, and Samsung Exynos) and diagnostic loggers is needed to separate baseband commit latencies from tool-specific write buffering.

\paragraph{More sessions and prospective public pre-registration.} Eight to ten independent sessions across days, times and routes would make a paired test over units informative, tighten the conformal feasibility floor and give the block bootstrap something to be conditional on. Furthermore, future campaigns should be registered in an external public registry prior to data acquisition to ensure that holdouts are blind to both model fitting and pipeline feature engineering, addressing the retrospective lag-repair limitation identified in Campaign 4.

\paragraph{A longer second-operator campaign and a third operator.} The 17-minute session of Section~\ref{sec:5_12} shows that the methodological findings transfer and that the learner ranking does not. It is too short to say which learner should be preferred across operators, or why. A multi-session campaign on the second operator, and a third operator, would turn that single data point into a between-operator comparison. They would also test the hypothesis that neighbour-gap features do not transfer between an A3-driven intra-frequency network and an A5-driven inter-frequency one, and they would break the single-configuration limitation, since the offsets reported in Section~\ref{sec:3_5} are one operator's choice rather than a property of LTE. Any learner chosen for cross-operator use should be selected on such data before it is evaluated, not on the session that exposed the problem.

\paragraph{An experimental test of the time-to-trigger.} Section~\ref{sec:5_10} withdraws the claim that the time-to-trigger is the lever governing ping-pong, because the configuration set does not vary anywhere in this data and the claim is therefore unsupported rather than merely uncertain. It remains the most plausible lever on theoretical grounds, and varying it under controlled conditions while measuring the resulting return rate is the direct test. Such an experiment would also supply the treatment variation that the causal design above requires, and would break the confounding between speed, route and cell density that four campaigns cannot.

\paragraph{Operational and calibration evidence the present tables do not contain.} Four analyses would turn the predictive signal into an operational argument, and all of them can be computed from the existing out-of-fold predictions: (i) recall evaluated against an explicit alarm budget and a minimum useful lead time for a named action such as target-cell preparation, with acquisition, export, feature-processing and inference delays measured or modelled; (ii) reliability curves with per-bin counts, calibration slope and intercept, and fold-specific constant-probability baselines; (iii) the event-level detection--false-alarm trade-off under 0, 1 and 2~s post-handover blanking; and (iv) the sensitivity of the A3 report--command attribution to the matching window and to a target-cell matching rule. An online adaptive conformal variant \cite{ref53}, evaluated sequentially across campaigns, would test a long-run guarantee that the present design cannot.

\paragraph{Release of code, data, and verifiable checksums.} The protocol and alignment arguments of Sections~\ref{sec:5_1} and~\ref{sec:5_3} are claims about how results in this field should be produced, and such claims are only worth making if others can reproduce and contest them --- Table~\ref{tab:2_1} grades 22 published models on exactly that property, so this work's own two rows in it have to say something specific. The alignment audit, the four evaluation protocols, the feature construction and the table-generation pipeline are kept in the project repository and are to be released as a versioned open-source archive with this thesis, together with the derived row-level frames, the decoded event streams, and the stage scripts. Appendix~\ref{app:C} documents the pipeline execution DAG, artifact layout, git provenance, and SHA-256 verification hashes for all derived frames. The raw vendor logs are not redistributable, which is stated rather than elided; the derived frames are sufficient to reproduce every number in Chapter 5.


\section{Concluding remark}
\label{sec:7_4}

The contribution of this work is not a new learning algorithm, and it is worth closing by saying so directly. Gradient boosting, discrete-time survival analysis and conformal risk control are all established methods, and this thesis is explicit throughout about where each came from. What it offers is measurement-grade ground truth decoded from signalling, a formulation that makes multi-horizon outputs coherent by construction, an evaluation protocol whose unit is defended and whose alternatives are measured, a conformal calibration diagnostic whose exchangeability assumption is examined in both regimes rather than assumed, and an alignment audit that removed this project's own most impressive result. On the evidence of the protocol audit in Section~\ref{sec:2_3}, the parts of that list the field most consistently omits are the least novel ones.
