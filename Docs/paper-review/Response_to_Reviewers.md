# Response to reviewers — *Handover forecasting from LTE drive-test signalling*

**Manuscript:** revised and retitled *Leakage-Audited Multi-Horizon Handover Forecasting from LTE Drive-Test Signalling*
**Date:** 18 September 2026 · reviewers alfa, bravo, charlie (meta-review of 17 September 2026)

We thank the three reviewers. The reviews were right on every count we could check, and one of
their concerns (C9, timestamp alignment) turned out to be worse than any reviewer suspected: it was
carrying most of the headline result. Everything numerical in the manuscript has been regenerated
from the pipeline; nothing is transcribed. The previous version of the manuscript is retained
unchanged for comparison.

## Common concerns

**C1 — the "drive" is a fixed 180-sample chunk, not an independent unit.** Accepted in full. Each
campaign is a single continuous logging session; the 57 blocks are slices of four sessions. §3.4 now
says so, §4.9 makes leave-one-campaign-out the primary protocol, and §5.3 measures what the other
protocols are worth (+13 % for random blocks, +48 % for
random rows). Blocked cross-validation with a 60 s purge is reported as the protocol to use when only
one session exists. Interval estimates are labelled as conditional on four sessions.

**C2 — the +74 % inflation was arithmetically impossible.** Withdrawn. The inflation table is now
per horizon, with absolute scores under all four protocols for every learner (Table 5.5) and the
per-horizon table in `c2_inflation_by_horizon.csv`.

**C3 — fold counts and p-value contradicted.** Withdrawn. There are four held-out campaigns; paired
comparisons are reported as four per-campaign differences with the exact sign-test p-value, which
cannot fall below 0.125. No p-value below that is quoted anywhere in the revision.

**C4 — hazard table and prevalence table did not reconcile.** Fixed. One common row set is used for
every horizon (rows with a full 5 s follow-up, outside the 2 s post-handover blank), so the hazards
chain exactly to the prevalences; Table 4.2 prints both columns side by side. The long-format size is
39,435 (sample, bin) pairs from 8,586 rows, not "approximately 34,000".

**C5 — the offset-sign claim contradicted Table 3.1.** Corrected and reversed. Weighted by
handovers, 75 % of A3-triggered commands follow a +1 dB profile. The
negative-offset configurations are long-interval re-reporting configurations; they are
neighbour-monitoring, not the mobility trigger. Contribution 4 has been rewritten accordingly.

**C6 — external dataset independence.** Corrected. §5.7 now states that the public dataset was
collected on the same operator, in the same city, with the same instrument licence and in the same
department, by different people on a different route two years earlier. It is called an independent
collection on the same network. Both domains are rebuilt from L3 signalling with identical feature
construction, and AUPRC, lift, ECE and bootstrap intervals are reported, not AUROC alone
(Table 5.9).

**C7 — A3 decline rate counted periodic repeats.** Accepted, and the correction needed a second
pass. Report configurations on this network carry reportInterval/reportAmount (the dominant one
re-reports every 240 ms, reportAmount infinity). Grouping reports of one measId into trigger
episodes gives 2,361 episodes from 8,136 reports
(3.4 per episode).

Our first revision then asked whether *some* command falls within two seconds of each episode and
reported 30 % declined. That is not a conversion rate. Several measIds
monitor different neighbours and fire during the same approach to a boundary, so the
2,361 episodes claim 1,647 commands between them out of the
957 that exist — the same command counted 2.6 times on average.
Matching each command to exactly one episode (the one holding the report it is attributed to under
the §3.5 rule) leaves 635 owning episodes, so 73 % are
declined. The loose rule also *rewards verbosity*: a configuration re-reporting on several
identities scores a lower decline rate because it is verbose, which inverts the reading the rate is
used for.

§5.9 now reports all three units side by side and makes the instability the finding, as §5.10
already does for ping-pong. What survives every unit — and sharpens under the strictest — is the
separation between the mobility configuration and the monitoring configurations:
36.9 % declined for the +1 dB / 320 ms profile against
95.2 % for the dominant negative-offset profile, where the loose rule gave
2.3 % against 49.5 %. The one well-defined direction is
the command side: 651 of 957 commands are attributed to an A3 report. The "rises
with cell size" claim is withdrawn — no cell-size measurement exists.

**C8 — dwell time and the burst alternative.** Accepted, and it changed the result. With the
alignment corrected, dwell time alone reaches AUROC 0.664, not 0.874. §5.8 adds
burst stratification (lift 3.1x on quiet rows against
1.9x inside bursts), a history-only baseline
(1.7x pooled), a Hawkes-kernel intensity baseline, and the dose-response plot of
dwell against handover probability.

**C9 — 1 Hz alignment leakage.** This was the decisive concern, and it is now checked on a second,
independent dataset (new Table A.3 and Section 5.1). The audit is now §5.1:
75.9 % of handovers already show their target cell as serving in the export row stamped
before the command (74.6 % for isolated handovers, against a background of
12.1 % three rows earlier). Every export feature is lagged one row; the one-second
AUPRC of the same model falls from 0.600 to 0.179. The unlagged numbers
are retained only as a measurement of the effect.

We also asked *why* the defect occurs, because a mechanism can be falsified and an anomaly cannot.
A row written at the end of its second carries the post-handover cell exactly when the handover
completes before that second closes — that is, when the margin m = 1 − δ − x is positive, for δ the
position of the command inside the second and x the execution and reporting delay. Our signalling
stream records x, so the prediction is directly testable: of the 15 handovers with
m < 0, **none** contaminates its row; of the 923 with m > 0, 77.1 % do.
The residual — 22.5 % of handovers where m > 0 and the row is nevertheless clean
— is the export refreshing its serving-cell field one row late, and row t + 1 carries the target in
84 % of those cases.

Two external datasets test the account rather than one.

*Control.* On the public 4G/5G Irish drive-test dataset of Raca et al. (MMSys 2020,
59 traces logged with G-NetTrack Pro, which samples the modem instead of aggregating
a second) row t is closer to the *old* cell at 29.4 % of 1,175
serving-cell changes, and the prediction gain from using row t rather than t − 1
(38 %) is smaller than the gain from t − 2 to t − 1 (57 %) —
the signature of a clean log.

*Replication with the rule under our control.* The NUWiNS multi-carrier corpus (PAM 2025) releases
raw XCAL exports at 10 Hz for 3 US tier-1 operators on two cross-country routes.
Downsampling it by keeping the **last** sample of each second reproduces the defect on
4,712 cell-changing handovers across 12 files: 93.2 % of rows stamped
t carry the target cell (92.8 % on the 3,160 isolated handovers), against
0.1 % at t − 1. Keeping the **first** sample of each second instead collapses this to
4.1 % on the very same handovers. One rule creates the defect, the other removes it,
with the network held fixed.

The conclusion we now draw is stronger and more useful than the one in the first revision: the
alignment error is a property of the row-aggregation rule, not of a vendor, a network or a region.
It should be audited wherever rows are aggregates, and the audit is a few lines against any event
clock.

One further result came out of this. Within the last fifth of a second, a row is contaminated
precisely when the serving-cell register updates before the second closes, so the contamination
share in a δ bin estimates the update-delay distribution. Over 903 such handovers it
runs 98 % with 75–100 ms left, 44 % with 50–75 ms left and
0 % below 50 ms. The alignment artefact, read deliberately, recovers a
sub-second quantity from a one-hertz export. This is reported in §5.1 and Table A.3.

**C10 — CRC evidence was one point.** Fixed. Table 5.7 reports five targets in two regimes:
calibration and test units drawn from the same pool of blocks (where exchangeability is plausible)
and calibration on one campaign with the guarantee claimed for another (where it is not). The
feasibility floor is presented as a corollary of Angelopoulos et al., not as a new rule, and the
"bound held on 82 % of drives" phrasing — which is not what CRC guarantees — is gone.

**C11 — the coherence comparison lacked the real control.** Accepted. Table 5.6 adds the per-row
cumulative-maximum projection and the PAV projection, both of which reach zero violations without a
held-out split, together with violation magnitudes, ECE, Brier and AUPRC for every arm. The claim for
the hazard formulation is now calibration and parsimony, not coherence alone. Equation (4.8) was
wrong and has been replaced: for small hazards the distortion is F̃ ≈ 1 − (1 − F)^w, which does not
compound as (1 + ε)^k.

**C12 — novelty over-attributed; Prognos omitted; audit unlisted.** Fixed. Prognos (inside ref. [5])
is discussed in §2.2 and §2.3 as the closest prior system. Conformalised survival analysis (Candès,
Lei and Ren) is cited in §2.5 and §2.6, and the title no longer claims it. All 22 audited models are
listed in the new Appendix D.

**C13 — timeline and freeze claim.** §3.2 now states the freeze honestly: recorded in the project's
own version history between 13 and 15 September 2026, not independently timestamped, and the
alignment audit post-dates all four campaigns, so no campaign is out of sample with respect to it.
The "0.933 before and after" argument is withdrawn.

**C14 — the mechanism claim.** Rewritten. What survives is that the serving-to-neighbour gap on
which A3 is defined is a weak predictor of imminence (AUROC 0.618 alone). What does
not survive is "the network triggers on its weakest signal" as a dwell-versus-gap contrast, because
the dwell figure was an alignment artefact. Hysteresis is now reported per profile, and
intra-frequency and inter-frequency handovers are separated.

**C15 — 341 re-establishments untreated.** Fixed. Causes were decoded:
321 reconfigurationFailure, 17 otherFailure,
3 handoverFailure, median 8 s after the previous
handover command. They are treated as a competing event: censoring the 93 affected
rows moves the one-second lift by +0.03x (Table 5.15 / §5.8).

**C16 — reference integrity.** The reference list was rebuilt from the verified bibliography and the
source PDFs. Corrected: [3] Deng et al. (title and given names), [4] Ghoshal et al. (title and full
author list from the arXiv front matter), [6] M2HO (title), [29] Deb et al. (title and six authors),
[33] the Mendeley dataset (title and version), and [34], which was never cited, removed. Three
entries still carry an explicit bracketed note because we could not verify the full author list from
a primary source; they are flagged rather than guessed.

**C17 — unfair sequence-model arm.** Fixed. Every learner now receives the identical feature matrix;
the sequence models consume windows of the same lagged feature vectors, including the history block.
All arms receive the same 20-trial random search, nested inside each outer fold and scored on a
purged calibration block.

**C18 — missing metrics; 0.811 vs 0.784; Table A.2 mislabel.** Fixed. False alarms per hour and per
kilometre and the lead-time distribution are in Table A.2; the operating point is named (5 % FPR,
threshold fitted in-fold) and is no longer confused with the conformal point. The signalling-block
ablation is reported once, from one table: 0.179 without it and 0.168
with it.

## Unique concerns

- **charlie, single-best-slice reporting.** Every table is now reported at all five horizons, and the
  external check reports AUPRC and ECE as well as AUROC.
- **alfa, VarMeasConfig semantics.** Corrected: identifiers persist until modified or removed; the
  earlier "scoped to the message" wording is withdrawn, and the parser now applies the removal lists
  (15,100 measId removals), the inter-frequency measId swap of clause 5.5.6.1
  (187 occurrences) and configuration release on leaving RRC_CONNECTED
  (56 occurrences). The fix is described as a bug fix, not a contribution.
- **alfa, left-truncated history.** Fixed: history features are computed over the whole session from
  signalling timestamps, not per 180 s block.
- **bravo, the A3 rule's −2 % inflation as a noise floor.** Adopted: the rule is now used explicitly
  as the zero-capacity control in the protocol ladder (+0 % across it).
- **bravo, 0.5 s is half of 1 s.** With the corrected alignment the 0.5 s row behaves as a bin of the
  hazard model rather than as a separate classifier; it is retained as bin 1 and reported with the
  caveat that it sits below the sample period.
- **charlie, the highway campaign inside the calibration pool.** Fixed: the conformal rotations hold
  out a whole campaign, and the highway campaign appears as a held-out campaign like the others.

## What we could not do

1. **More independent units.** Four sessions is what exists. Every interval and every paired test is
   reported with that limit stated; we do not claim significance from four units.
2. **A second operator or radio access technology.** Not available within this project.
3. **Exact row-time semantics.** The audit shows a one-row lag is necessary and sufficient on the
   evidence available, and the public-dataset comparison shows the defect is instrument-specific, but
   the vendor does not document what the row timestamp means. A native-rate re-export would settle
   it and is the first item of future work.
4. **A faithful Prognos baseline.** No implementation is public; it is discussed as related work and
   not reimplemented.
